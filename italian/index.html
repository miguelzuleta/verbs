Italian Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Italian translation and every conjugation in the table.

Compound tenses use avere or essere as the auxiliary (ho parlato, sono andato). With essere the participle agrees with the subject, so both endings are shown (andato/a, andati/e). The passato prossimo is the everyday past in speech; the passato remoto is used in writing and storytelling, and in everyday speech mainly in the South and in Tuscany. Lei is the formal "you" and takes the 3rd-person singular form, so it shares the lui / lei / Lei button. The imperative has no io form (that button shows the tu form), and its Lei and Loro forms are the polite ones. A dash (—) marks a form that does not exist: weather verbs such as piovere only have the lui / lei / Lei form.
