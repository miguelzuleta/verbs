Swahili Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Swahili translation and every conjugation in the table.

A Swahili verb is one word built from a subject prefix (ni-, u-, a-, tu-, m-, wa-), a tense marker (-na-, -li-, -me-, -ta-, -ki-, -nge-, -ngali-, -ja-, -ku-), the root and a final vowel. The pronoun (mimi, wewe ...) is only used for emphasis, so each cell shows just the verb. Verbs whose root has one syllable (kula, kunywa, kuja) keep ku- after -na-, -li-, -me-, -ta-, -nge- and -ngali- (ninakula, nitakula) but drop it in the habitual, the subjunctive, -ki-, -ja- and the negative present (hula, nile, nikila, sijala, sili). The negative uses a different subject prefix (si-, hu-, ha-, hatu-, ham-, hawa-), and the final -a changes in some tenses: to -i in the negative present (sizungumzi) and to -e in the subjunctive (nizungumze). Verbs borrowed from Arabic that end in -i, -u or -e (kujibu, kukubali, kusamehe) keep that vowel in the negative present and the subjunctive (sijibu, nijibu). The habitual (hu-) has no subject prefix, so every button shows the same word. The imperative has only wewe and ninyi forms (the other buttons show —). The verb forms shown are for people (the human noun class); other noun classes use other prefixes.
