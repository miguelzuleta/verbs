Hindi Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Hindi translation and how to say it in every tense.

Hindi verb forms agree with the gender of the subject, so each cell shows the masculine form, a slash, then the feminine form (a single form when gender makes no difference, as in the subjunctive). Every cell shows the Devanagari with its romanization underneath; in the romanization ṁ marks a nasal vowel and ā ī ū are long vowels. The stem is the infinitive without ना (बोल in बोलना). तू is intimate (used with children and very close friends, rude to others), तुम is familiar and आप is respectful; हम, तुम, आप and वे take the plural forms. In the simple past of a verb that takes ने, the subject gets ने and the verb agrees with the object, not the subject, so the table shows ने plus the masculine singular form (ने बोला) for every person; the two perfect tenses work the same way. When the object noun in the entry is feminine, the feminine form is shown (ने बात की); when it is plural, the plural form (ने पैसे दिए, ने आहें भरीं). Compound verbs (noun + करना, such as बात करना) are conjugated on the last word only. Nouns in compound verbs keep their full spelling in the romanization (yojanā), and only verb stems drop the schwa as spoken (samjhā). A compound with होना that names a state is shown with the copula (मैं हकदार हूँ), so the tenses that would mean "become" show a dash; verbs where the noun is the subject (मुझे ज़रूरत है, मुझे डर लगता है) only have the third-person forms (वह and वे), and the other pills show a dash. The imperative exists only for तू, तुम, आप and (as "let us") हम; the other buttons show a dash.

For what is happening right now, the continuous is more usual.
Indicative Present Continuous (अपूर्ण वर्तमान) Stem + रहा / रहे / रही (rahā / rahe / rahī) + the present of होना (हूँ / है / हैं / हो) बोल रहा हूँ / बोल रही हूँbol rahā hūṁ / bol rahī hūṁ Actions in progress now ("I am speaking"); also something about to happen ("I'm coming")
Indicative Simple Past (सामान्य भूतकाल) Stem + ā / e / ī / īṁ, agreeing with the subject: बोला, बोले, बोली, बोलीं (masculine singular, masculine plural, feminine singular, feminine plural); after a vowel ā becomes yā (खाया). Irregular: किया, गया, हुआ, दिया, लिया
With ने: subject + ने + the participle agreeing with the object (shown masculine singular, or feminine or plural when the object noun in the entry is)		 ने बोलाne bolā Completed actions in the past ("I spoke", "he went")
Indicative Past Habitual (आभ्यासिक भूत) Stem + ता / ते / ती + था / थे / थी / थीं (thā / the / thī / thīṁ) बोलता था / बोलती थीboltā thā / boltī thī Habits and repeated actions in the past ("used to speak"); also states that lasted for a time
Indicative Past Continuous (अपूर्ण भूत) Stem + रहा / रहे / रही + था / थे / थी / थीं बोल रहा था / बोल रही थीbol rahā thā / bol rahī thī An action in progress at a moment in the past ("I was speaking")
Indicative Present Perfect (आसन्न भूत) Past participle (as in the simple past) + हूँ / है / हैं / हो
With ने: subject + ने + participle + है		 ने बोला हैne bolā hai A past action that has just happened or whose result still holds ("I have spoken")
Indicative Past Perfect (पूर्ण भूत) Past participle + था / थे / थी / थीं
With ने: subject + ने + participle + था (थी with a feminine object)		 ने बोला थाne bolā thā An action completed before another moment in the past, or long ago ("I had spoken")
Indicative Future (सामान्य भविष्यत्) Present subjunctive + गा / गे / गी (gā / ge / gī): masculine singular, masculine plural, feminine बोलूँगा / बोलूँगीbolūṁgā / bolūṁgī Plans, predictions and promises ("I will speak"); also a guess about the present (वह घर पर होगा "he must be at home")
Subjunctive Subjunctive (संभावनार्थ) Stem + ūṁ (मैं), e (तू, वह), eṁ (हम, आप, वे), o (तुम): बोलूँ, बोले, बोलें, बोलो; no gender बोलूँbolūṁ Wishes, suggestions, possibility and purpose; offers and questions such as "Shall I speak?"
Imperative Imperative (आज्ञा) तू: the stem (बोल). तुम: stem + o (बोलो). आप: stem + iye (बोलिए; irregular कीजिए, दीजिए, लीजिए, पीजिए). हम: the subjunctive, "let us" (बोलें) — Commands, requests and invitations: तू is blunt or intimate, तुम familiar, आप polite; हम means "let us"
Imperative Negative Imperative (निषेध) मत (mat) + the imperative of तू, तुम or आप; no हम form. In a compound verb मत stands before the last word — Telling someone not to do something ("Don't speak"); the negative of the imperative is मत, not नहीं
