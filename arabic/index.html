Arabic Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Arabic translation and how to say it in every tense.

Arabic is written right to left. Everyday Arabic leaves out the short vowels, but this page writes every verb form fully vowelled, and every form comes with a transliteration underneath (ʾ is the hamza, ʿ is the letter ع ʿayn, ā ī ū are long vowels, and a doubled letter such as ll marks a shadda: كَلَّمَ kallama). Arabic verbs change for person, for gender (أنتَ you, m. / أنتِ you, f.; هو he / هي she) and for number, including the DUAL (exactly two people: أنتما "you two", هما "they two"), which is why there are thirteen buttons, not six; the two هما buttons have the same pronoun, but the verb shows the gender (كَتَبَا (katabā) m., كَتَبَتَا (katabatā) f.). Each verb is built from a root (three or four consonants that carry the basic meaning) and a form (I to X) that changes the meaning: II intensive or causative, III does something to someone, IV causative, V reflexive of II, VI reciprocal, VII passive-like, VIII reflexive, IX colours and defects, X asks for or considers. The present tense (the imperfect) has three moods: indicative, subjunctive and jussive. The imperative only exists for you (أنتَ أنتِ أنتما أنتم أنتنّ); the other buttons show —. The participles do not change with the person: they are shown in the masculine singular.

Indicative Past (الماضي) Past stem + ending for the person: -tu (I), -ta / -ti (you, m. / f.), -tumā (you two), -tum / -tunna (you, m. / f. pl.), -nā (we); he: -a, she: -at; they two: -ā (m.) / -atā (f.); they: -ū (m.) / -na (f.) تَكَلَّمْتُtakallamtu Completed actions in the past ("spoke", "has spoken"). The he-form (كَتَبَ (kataba)) is the dictionary form of the verb
Indicative Present (المضارع) Prefix (أَ a-, تَ ta-, يَ ya-, نَ na-; u- instead of a- in forms II, III, IV and Q1) + stem + ending: -u (singular), -īna (you, f.), -āni (dual), -ūna (m. pl.), -na (f. pl.): يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu) أَتَكَلَّمُatakallamu Actions happening now, habits and general truths ("speaks", "is speaking"); with a time word it can also mean the future
Indicative Future (المستقبل) سَ (sa-) + present, written joined to the verb: سَيَكْتُبُ (sa-yaktubu) سَأَتَكَلَّمُsa-atakallamu Actions that will happen later ("will speak"). سَوْفَ (sawfa) + present is another way to say it
Subjunctive Subjunctive (المضارع المنصوب) Present with -u replaced by -a (يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu) → يَكْتُبَ (yaktuba)); -ūna, -āni and -īna lose their final -na (تَكْتُبُونَ (taktubūna) → تَكْتُبُوا (taktubū)); the f. pl. keeps -na أَتَكَلَّمَatakallama After a particle (not shown here): أَنْ (an, "to", "that"), لِ (li-, "in order to"), كَيْ (kay, "so that"), حَتَّى (ḥattā, "until"); also after "want", "must", "try": أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَتَكَلَّمَ (urīdu an atakallama, "I want to speak")
Jussive Jussive (المضارع المجزوم) Present with the final vowel dropped (sukūn): يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu) → يَكْتُبْ (yaktub); -ūna, -āni and -īna lose their final -na; the f. pl. keeps -na; hollow and defective verbs shorten the last vowel (يَقُولُ (yaqūlu) → يَقُلْ (yaqul); يَرْمِي (yarmī) → يَرْمِ (yarmi)) أَتَكَلَّمْatakallam After لَمْ (lam, "did not"); after لَا (lā) for "do not ...!"; after لِ (li-) for "let him / let us ..."; in conditions (إِنْ in, "if"; مَنْ man, "whoever"). The particle is not shown here
Imperative Imperative (الأمر) The jussive of you without its prefix (تَكْتُبْ (taktub) → اُكْتُبْ (uktub)); a helping alif (hamzat al-waṣl, اِ / اُ) is added when the word would begin with two consonants, and form IV begins with a spoken hamza (أَكْرِمْ akrim); endings: -ī (you, f.), -ā (you two), -ū (you, m. pl.), -na (you, f. pl.) — Telling someone to do something; only for you (أنتَ أنتِ أنتما أنتم أنتنّ), so the other buttons show —. A negative command is لَا + jussive (لَا تَكْتُبْ (lā taktub), "do not write")
Negative Negative Past (لم + المجزوم) لَمْ (lam) + jussive: لَمْ يَكْتُبْ (lam yaktub) لَمْ أَتَكَلَّمْlam atakallam Actions that did not happen ("did not speak", "has not spoken"). The jussive looks like a present form, but the meaning is past
Negative Negative Present (لا + المضارع) لَا (lā) + present: لَا يَكْتُبُ (lā yaktubu) لَا أَتَكَلَّمُlā atakallamu Actions that are not happening or do not usually happen ("does not speak", "is not speaking")
Negative Negative Future (لن + المنصوب) لَنْ (lan) + subjunctive: لَنْ يَكْتُبَ (lan yaktuba) لَنْ أَتَكَلَّمَlan atakallama Actions that will not happen ("will not speak")
Passive voice Past Passive (الماضي المبني للمجهول) Past with the vowels changed: the vowel before the last root letter becomes i, the ones before it become u (كَتَبَ (kataba) → كُتِبَ (kutiba)); same endings as the active past تُكُلِّمْتُtukullimtu What was done to someone or something, without saying who did it ("was written", "was said"). Passive voice Present Passive (المضارع المبني للمجهول) Present with the prefix vowel changed to u and the vowel before the last root letter to a: يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu) → يُكْتَبُ (yuktabu) أُتَكَلَّمُutakallamu What is being done to someone or something, without saying who does it ("is being written"); also general facts ("Arabic is spoken here")
Participles Active Participle (اسم الفاعل) Form I: the pattern فَاعِلٌ (fāʿilun): كَاتِبٌ (kātibun); other forms: mu- in place of the present prefix, with a kasra before the last root letter: مُكَلِّمٌ (mukallimun); masculine singular, ending -un مُتَكَلِّمٌmutakallimun The doer of the action, used as a noun or an adjective ("writer", "speaking"); often describes a state or something happening now
Participles Passive Participle (اسم المفعول) Form I: the pattern مَفْعُولٌ (mafʿūlun): مَكْتُوبٌ (maktūbun); other forms: mu- in place of the present prefix, with a fatḥa before the last root letter: مُكَلَّمٌ (mukallamun); masculine singular, ending -un مُتَكَلَّمٌmutakallamun The thing or person that undergoes the action, used as an adjective or a noun ("written", "spoken").
