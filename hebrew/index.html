Hebrew Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Hebrew translation and how to say it in every tense.

Hebrew is written right to left and without most vowels, so every form comes with a transliteration underneath (kh is the sound of ח, ts of צ); an apostrophe marks א or ע between vowels (sho'el); the transliteration marks no stress. Hebrew verbs change for gender as well as person, which is why there are ten buttons, not six: אתה (you, masc.) and את (you, fem.) differ, and so do אתם / אתן and הם / הן. The present tense has only four forms (the participle, which agrees in gender and number), so several buttons show the same word; for אני and אנחנו the present tense shows the masculine form, and a woman speaking uses the feminine one (כותבת, כותבות). The imperative only exists for you (אתה / את / אתם / אתן); the other buttons show —. The infinitive is the same for every person.
