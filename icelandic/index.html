Icelandic Verb Tenses

Look up an English verb to see its Icelandic translation and every conjugation in the table.

Icelandic verbs agree with their subject in person and number; the six buttons show the verb without the pronoun. Strong verbs change their vowel (taka, tók, tekið) and weak verbs add a dental ending (heyra, heyrði, heyrt). A vowel change called u-umlaut turns a into ö before -um (tölum, töluðum). Every compound tense uses hafa with the supine (hef talað); the future is munu + infinitive. The imperative has no ég, hann / hún / það or þeir / þær / þau form (those buttons show —), and its 2nd person singular ends in -ðu / -du / -tu (talaðu, sendu, taktu). Some verbs take an object-like subject in the dative or accusative instead of the nominative (mig langar "I want", mér finnst "I think"); this table does not show that. A dash (—) marks a form that does not exist: weather verbs such as rigna only have the hann / hún / það form, and modal verbs such as mega have no imperative.

Indicative Present (Nútíð) Simple tala Current actions, habits and general truths; with a time word it also refers to the future (Ég fer á morgun)
Indicative Past (Þátíð) Simple: weak verbs add a dental ending (-aði, -ði, -di, -ti), strong verbs change their stem vowel talaði Completed past actions and the narrative past (stories, news, everyday speech); there is no separate imperfect, so it also covers "was speaking" and "used to speak"
Indicative Perfect (Núliðin tíð) hafa (pres.) + supine hef talað A past action that still matters now, or experience up to now ("I have spoken"); with a stated past time (yesterday, in 2010) Icelandic uses the simple past instead
Indicative Past Perfect (Þáliðin tíð) hafa (past) + supine hafði talað An action completed before another moment in the past ("I had spoken")
Indicative Future (Framtíð) munu (pres.) + infinitive mun tala Predictions, promises and plans; also a likely guess about the present. Everyday speech often uses the present with a time word instead
Indicative Future Perfect munu (pres.) + hafa + supine mun hafa talað An action that will be finished by a future moment; also a likely guess about the past ("he will have left")
Subjunctive Present Subjunctive (Viðtengingarháttur nútíðar) Simple tali Wishes and blessings in set phrases (Guð blessi Ísland); reported speech and wishes in subordinate clauses after a present-tense main verb (Hún segir að hann sé veikur, Ég vil að hann komi)
Subjunctive Past Subjunctive (Viðtengingarháttur þátíðar) Simple: weak verbs usually look like the past indicative; strong verbs use the past plural stem with i-umlaut and -i (tókum > tæki) talaði Unreal wishes and conditions (Ef ég væri ríkur "if I were rich"); reported speech after a past-tense main verb (Hún sagði að hann væri veikur)
Subjunctive Perfect Subjunctive hafa (pres. subj.) + supine hafi talað Reported speech or doubt about something already done, after a present-tense main verb (Hún segir að hann hafi farið)
Subjunctive Past Perfect Subjunctive hafa (past subj.) + supine hefði talað Unreal past situations (Ef ég hefði vitað það "if I had known it") and regrets; reported speech after a past-tense main verb
Conditional Conditional myndi + infinitive myndi tala What would happen under a condition ("I would speak if ..."), and polite or tentative statements; the past subjunctive can do the same job
Conditional Conditional Perfect myndi + hafa + supine myndi hafa talað What would have happened if things had been different ("I would have spoken"); the past perfect subjunctive (hefði talað) can do the same job
Imperative Imperative (Boðháttur) Simple: the þú form ends in -ðu / -du / -tu; við and þið use the present forms — Telling someone to do something (only þú, við and þið; við means "let's")
